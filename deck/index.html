Sound design, done by something that can't hear.

An agent that programs an FM synthesizer from a sample or a sentence — and checks its own work with a spectrum analyser instead of ears.

Where it comes from1967 · Stanford

Vibrato, pushed past hearing, becomes timbre.

Past roughly 20 Hz the ear stops following the wobble, and it collapses into a new tone. Huge range, almost no computation.

The tell1983 · Yamaha DX7
200,000+units · and almost nobody programmed one

The presets were good, so the presets became the ceiling. The best-selling synth ever made turned into a preset machine.

The taskIntention → parameters

There is no knob marked brighter.

Subtractive gives you a monotonic dial you can steer by ear. FM gives you three coupled parameters where moving one changes what the others mean.

The job, taken apartFour moves

Four moves, and an agent that makes all of them.

  • 1 · intendknow what you want — a sample, or a sentence
  • 2 · translateknow the engine — which parameter moves which quality
  • 3 · evaluatehear what came out — except it can't
  • 4 · iterateattribute the change, adjust
1 · intendA sample, or a sentence

The input is what you want, not what to turn.

Drop in any audio — it needn't be an FM sound — or describe it. Nothing about operators, ratios, or envelopes.

“a dark rubbery bass with a short percussive thump” ↓ Rubber Thumb Bass harmonicity 1 modulationIndex 5.5 carrierFm ratio 0.5 · index 1.8 ampEnv .004 / .16 / .22 / .12
actual session output
2 · translateThe causal model

Someone has to know that modEnv is brightness over time.

The agent is handed the engine's causal model — index is brightness, integer ratios are harmonic, non-integer ratios ring like metal — and reasons from it.

“…a very short modEnv decay to sustain 0.05, so the brightness is only a percussive thump at the front. Sub-ish carrierFm at ratio 0.5 adds rubbery body.” the agent, explaining Rubber Thumb Bass
3 · evaluateNo ears · so, measurement

It can't listen. So it measures.

An STFT reduces any sound to twelve harmonics at four moments in time, plus pitch, noisiness and brightness. Small enough to reason about.

The ruleCompass, not score

The number tells it which way to walk. Not when to be happy.

FM will never become a sampled upright bass, and chasing the last few points makes the sound worse. So it's told to prefer the sound — and to finalise the patch it would rather play.

“more thumb-pluck bite” you
“Now there's a real thumb snap: attack brightness jumps to 3.7× and collapses within 45 ms onto the same rubbery body.” it
